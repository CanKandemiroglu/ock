01Research Profile

I combine organic geochemical analysis, stable carbon-isotope tracing, and microbial cultivation to investigate carbon cycling in marine and subsurface environments.

My work has spanned 13C-labelling experiments on deep-sea sediments with compound-specific isotope analysis of fatty acids and intact polar lipids; MALDI-FT-ICR-MS imaging of lipid biomarkers in microbial mats as early-Earth analogues; and the design of experimental model systems for bacterial degradation of organic matter. I am particularly interested in characterising chemoautotrophic metabolic pathways in CO2-rich subsurface ecosystems and in identifying molecular biosignatures of ancient carbon fixation.

02Research Interests

Microbial Biogeochemistry

How microbial communities transform carbon in sediments and the deep biosphere, resolved through isotope-labelling and lipid analysis.

Stable Isotope Geochemistry

Compound-specific δ13C tracing to quantify metabolic activity and distinguish bacterial from archaeal pathways.

Molecular Biosignatures

Lipid biomarkers and their spatial distribution as records of past life — with relevance to early-Earth and astrobiology.

Organic Matter Cycling

Experimental model systems linking laboratory degradation rates to carbon flux in the ocean.

04Selected Research Experience

10/2024 – 12/2025 · University of Bremen & MPI Bremen

Master's Thesis — Organic Geochemistry & Marine Glycobiochemistry

Supervisors: Prof. K.-U. Hinrichs & Dr. J.-H. Hehemann

  • Designed a marine minimal medium with systematic nutrient gradients for controlled bacterial growth experiments.
  • Cultured six marine Vibrio strains in mono- and co-culture; monitored growth dynamics over 48–96 h in biological triplicates.
  • Synthesised alginate microparticles and developed real-time degradation monitoring via time-lapse microscopy and spectrophotometry.
  • Translated laboratory degradation rates into water-column carbon-flux predictions.

08/2023 – 09/2024 · University of Bremen

Microbial Mat Biogeochemistry — Light-Driven Molecular Structuring

Supervisor: Dr. Benjamin Nettersheim

  • Developed MALDI-FT-ICR-MS imaging workflows for extraction-free detection of intact polar lipids, pigments and carotenoids at 50 µm resolution in hypersaline microbial mats.
  • Identified novel biomarker signatures and 14 previously uncharacterised compounds associated with sulfate-reducing bacteria.
  • Integrated MALDI-MSI, µ-XRF elemental mapping and microsensor data into a five-zone redox framework, with explicit relevance to biosignature preservation.
