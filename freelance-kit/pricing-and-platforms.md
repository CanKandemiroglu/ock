# Platforms & Pricing — Where to Sign Up and What to Charge

All free to join. Ranked by fit for your profile.

## Tier 1 — Sign up first (highest fit, best rates)

### Kolabtree — kolabtree.com
Built specifically for PhD freelancers. Clients want specialists; your niche is an asset, not a liability.
- **Services:** data analysis, statistical review, manuscript editing, scientific consulting, literature search.
- **Typical rates:** $30–150/hr for PhDs; experienced consultants far higher.
- **Your move:** use `profile-kolabtree.md`, list a $45–60/hr rate, bid on open projects daily.

### Upwork — upwork.com
Biggest marketplace; huge volume of editing + data jobs. Reviews compound here.
- **Your move:** use `profile-upwork.md`. Start $40/hr. Bid with `proposal-templates.md`. Do 1–2 cheap fixed-price gigs first for reviews, then raise.

### Freelance scientific-editor pools (apply directly)
These agencies hire subject-expert editors and feed you steady work (you don't have to hunt clients):
- **Cactus Communications / Editage** — freelance scientific editors ~$44/hr equivalent.
- **Enago**, **Wordvice**, **AJE (American Journal Experts)**, **Charlesworth** — similar.
- **Your move:** apply with Template E + your CV. Expect a short editing test. Being a *domain scientist* + bilingual makes you stand out.

## Tier 2 — Add these for volume

### Fiverr Pro — fiverr.com
Post the 5 gigs from `service-listings.md`. Fiverr Pro badge (apply for it — your credentials qualify) commands higher prices and trust.

### Preply / Superprof — tutoring
Chemistry, geoscience, statistics, and languages (German, Turkish, English). €20–35/hr, flexible, fast first students. Great for filling your 15 hrs/week and steady cash between bigger gigs.

## Pricing strategy (the review flywheel)

1. **First 5 reviews are everything.** Price your entry gigs low enough that saying yes is a no-brainer ($20–35 starter tiers). Over-deliver. Ask happy clients for a review every time.
2. **Then raise 30–50%.** Once you have social proof, your PhD-level rates ($55–75+/hr, or $0.06–0.10/word for editing) become believable.
3. **Anchor with tiers.** Always offer 3 options — most clients pick the middle. Your "Premium" tier makes "Standard" look reasonable.
4. **Bill value, not hours, where you can.** "A publication-ready figure set — $140" beats "$40/hr" once you're fast.

## Realistic first-month math (15 hrs/week, $0 spent)

| Scenario | What it looks like | Monthly |
|---|---|---|
| Slow start | 1 editing pool + a few small gigs while reviews build | $300–800 |
| Traction | Steady editing pool work + 3–4 Upwork/Kolabtree gigs | $1,000–2,500 |
| Rolling | Repeat clients + higher rates + tutoring filler | $2,500–5,000+ |

The scientists who earn most treat freelancing like research: a repeatable pipeline (apply daily → deliver → collect review → raise rate → get repeat clients). Your credentials are rare. Price like it once the reviews back you up.
