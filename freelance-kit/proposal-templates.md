# Proposal / Cover-Letter Templates

The first line matters most — clients skim. Lead with the specific match, not "Dear Sir/Madam". Keep proposals short. Send many.

---

## Template A — Manuscript / thesis editing job
> Hi [Name],
>
> I'd be glad to edit your [manuscript / thesis] on [their topic]. I'm a marine geoscientist (M.Sc., University of Bremen) and I edit scientific writing in [their field-adjacent area] — so I'll sharpen the language *and* catch the scientific imprecisions reviewers flag (overclaims, vague stats, inconsistent terms), not just grammar.
>
> One thing I noticed from your description: [specific observation — e.g. "you're targeting a high-impact journal, so I'd focus on tightening the abstract and framing the gap"].
>
> I can turn around [scope] by [realistic date]. Native-level English and German. Your unpublished work stays strictly confidential.
>
> Happy to do a short sample edit of one paragraph so you can judge the fit — no charge. Shall I?
>
> Best, Osman

## The "free sample" close (use it a lot)
Offering a tiny free sample (one edited paragraph, one figure, a 10-minute data look) removes the client's risk and wins jobs — especially before you have reviews. It costs you 10 minutes and converts far better than talking about your qualifications.
